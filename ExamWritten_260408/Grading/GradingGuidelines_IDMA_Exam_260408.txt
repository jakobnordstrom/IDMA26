GENERAL COMMENTS

Please note that just giving the right answer is never enough unless the problem statement explicitly says so. Normally, just stating a correct solution without explanation gives zero points. Students have to provide explanations of how they solved the problems and why their solutions are correct. Just referring to a figure or table or whatever is not enough (although if some kind of material that partly explains the answer has been given, then this material should be graded and can give points).


PROBLEM 1: MATHEMATICAL INDUCTION

GENERAL GUIDELINES FOR BOTH SUBPROBLEMS:
- Identifies that induction is needed; nothing more: 5 p
- No mention at all of base case: -10 p
- Base case clearly mentioned, but never verified (not even pointed out as obvious): - 5 p
- Unclear where/how induction hypothesis is used (important to state clearly): -10 p
- Missing details, but broad outlines OK (clear base case, IH use explicit): -5 p
- Doing several base cases (unnecessarily) but whole proof as such is OK: no deduction
- Base case correct, but nothing else: 5 p
- Base case correct; induction hypothesis stated; but no further progress: 10 p
- Base case wrong, induction hypothesis stated correctly; but no further progress: 5 p
- Induction step fully correct, but serious issues with base case or missing first case: 20 p
- Induction step argues from n+1 to n: -10 p

PROBLEM 1a (30 p)
Missing base case n=1: -5 p
Intuitive proof sketch but without setting up any induction: 15 p

PROBLEM 1b (30 p)
Not enough base cases to make induction go through: -15 p
Sets up sufficient number of base cases, but fails to carry out induction step: 15 p
OK inductive proof, but for a and b potentially negative: 15 p
